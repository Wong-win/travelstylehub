<p>The Infatuation has spent more than a decade reviewing restaurants across the United States, and its annual lists have become one of the more reliable shortcuts for travellers who want to eat well without spending weeks on research. The selection below brings together some of the highest-rated rooms in the country, from a twelve-seat counter in San Francisco to a formal tasting menu in Chicago.</p>

<p>What connects them is not a single style of cooking but a level of intent: these are places where the kitchen is clearly trying to do something specific, whether that is refining a family recipe, working with a single region's ingredients, or building a menu around a personal story. That also means most of them are small, popular, and hard to book. Plan ahead, and treat the reservation as part of the trip rather than an afterthought.</p>

<p><strong>Tatiana – New York City</strong></p>
<p><strong>Cuisine</strong>: American<br />
<strong>Location</strong>: Upper West Side, New York<br />
<strong>Ideal for</strong>: Special occasions, romantic evenings, impressing visitors, birthdays<br />
<strong>Description</strong>: Located at Lincoln Center, Tatiana offers an ambiance reminiscent of 90s nightclubs with blue and gold lighting, music from Lauryn Hill and Biggie, and innovative cuisine. The signature dish is the short rib suya pastrami, served with caraway coconut bread, inviting you to create sliders. Reservations are highly sought after, but the wait is worth it for a revolutionary dining experience in New York.</p>

<p>Chef Kwame Onwuachi's cooking draws on his Nigerian, Jamaican, and Bronx background, and the menu reads like a personal map of the city rather than a fixed cuisine. The room is loud, warm, and deliberately festive — this is not a quiet dinner. Book as soon as reservations open, and expect a cancellation policy.</p>

<p><strong>Anajak Thai – Los Angeles</strong></p>
<p><strong>Cuisine</strong>: Thai<br />
<strong>Location</strong>: Sherman Oaks, Los Angeles<br />
<strong>Ideal for</strong>: Unique dining experiences, outdoor patio, drinking fine wine, impressing visitors<br />
<strong>Description</strong>: This family-run restaurant offers a dining experience that varies with each visit. Tuesdays are reserved for dry fish tacos with guest chefs, while other evenings feature southern Thai fried chicken and unique wines. The outdoor omakase is particularly popular. Anajak has become the favorite restaurant of many LA residents.</p>

<p>The restaurant is run by the Saengsawang family, and the menu moves between the Thai dishes they grew up with and a wine list that is unusually serious for the neighbourhood. The Tuesday format and the outdoor omakase are the two experiences most people come for, and both require booking. It is a good example of a restaurant that has kept its local identity while becoming a destination.</p>

<p><strong>Ambra – Philadelphia</strong></p>
<p><strong>Cuisine</strong>: Italian<br />
<strong>Location</strong>: Queen Village, Philadelphia<br />
<strong>Ideal for</strong>: Drinking fine wine, special occasions, unique dining experiences<br />
<strong>Description</strong>: This intimate, tasting-menu-only restaurant feels more like a dinner among friends than a traditional restaurant. With rustic Italian cuisine served with extreme precision and a touch of molecular gastronomy, Ambra offers exceptional service and a warm atmosphere. The three-hour meals are filled with lively conversations and exquisite dishes.</p>

<p>Because there is only one menu and a small number of seats, the evening has the rhythm of a dinner party rather than a restaurant service. The kitchen's Italian references are recognisable, but the plating and technique are modern, and the wine pairing is a big part of the experience. Book well in advance and arrive hungry — three hours is not an exaggeration.</p>

<p><strong>Tâm Tâm – Miami</strong></p>
<p><strong>Cuisine</strong>: Vietnamese<br />
<strong>Location</strong>: Downtown, Miami<br />
<strong>Ideal for</strong>: Romantic evenings, special occasions, outdoor patio<br />
<strong>Description</strong>: Originally a pop-up supper club, Tâm Tâm continues to provide an exceptional social and culinary experience. Signature dishes include caramelized chicken wings with fish sauce and pork ribs glazed with tamarind. Even the bathrooms are a fun experience not to be missed.</p>

<p>The room is designed for sharing and for a long evening, with a menu that leans on punchy Vietnamese flavours and plenty of cocktails. It is one of the more sociable restaurants in the downtown area, and the atmosphere is closer to a dinner party than a formal restaurant. Book ahead for weekends.</p>

<p><strong>Oriole – Chicago</strong></p>
<p><strong>Cuisine</strong>: American<br />
<strong>Location</strong>: Fulton Market, Chicago<br />
<strong>Ideal for</strong>: Birthdays, romantic evenings, fine dining, special occasions<br />
<strong>Description</strong>: Oriole offers a 12-course tasting menu that justifies its high price tag. With a discreet entrance and an unpretentious atmosphere, attentive servers present dishes that redefine culinary expectations. The foie gras mousse with blueberries on toasted brioche is a highlight of the meal, enjoyed by guests directly at the open kitchen.</p>

<p>The dining room is split across levels and built around an open kitchen, so you can watch the plating as dishes are finished. Service is polished but relaxed, and the pacing is designed so that a twelve-course menu does not feel rushed. It is one of the defining fine-dining experiences in the city, and reservations open well in advance.</p>

<p><strong>Noodle In A Haystack – San Francisco</strong></p>
<p><strong>Cuisine</strong>: Japanese<br />
<strong>Location</strong>: Richmond, San Francisco<br />
<strong>Ideal for</strong>: Eating at the bar, special occasions, unique dining experiences<br />
<strong>Description</strong>: This 12-seat restaurant, formerly a pop-up, is run by a charming couple who serve inspired Japanese dishes. The ramen dishes, always impeccably prepared, change regularly. Reservations are hard to come by, but the experience is well worth the wait.</p>

<p>With only a dozen seats, the meal is essentially a conversation with the people cooking it. The menu is built around ramen and small Japanese plates, but the details change constantly, so returning diners rarely eat the same thing twice. It is one of the hardest reservations in the city; set a reminder for when bookings open.</p>

<p><strong>Archipelago – Seattle</strong></p>
<p><strong>Cuisine</strong>: Filipino<br />
<strong>Location</strong>: Hillman City, Seattle<br />
<strong>Ideal for</strong>: Romantic evenings, drinking fine wine, quiet meals, special occasions, unique dining experiences, impressing visitors<br />
<strong>Description</strong>: This eight-seat restaurant offers a 10-course dinner inspired by the owners' Filipino heritage and Pacific Northwest ingredients. Each dish tells a story, connecting Filipino culture to the Seattle region. The dining experience is complemented by a warm atmosphere and attentive service.</p>

<p>Eight seats means the whole evening is essentially hosted, with the cooks explaining dishes as they arrive. The menu is a genuine blend of two places — Filipino flavours built around what is available in the Pacific Northwest — and the small scale makes it feel personal rather than theatrical. Book as early as you can.</p>

<p><strong>Lazy Betty – Atlanta</strong></p>
<p><strong>Cuisine</strong>: Experimental<br />
<strong>Location</strong>: Midtown, Atlanta<br />
<strong>Ideal for</strong>: Unique dining experiences, special occasions, fine dining<br />
<strong>Description</strong>: Lazy Betty is known for its innovative tasting menus that change frequently. The restaurant offers a variety of seasonal dishes, ranging from tender cod fillet to a reimagined beef Wellington. The decor with live plants and elegant lighting creates a welcoming ambiance for a memorable evening.</p>

<p>The kitchen treats the tasting menu as a changing project rather than a fixed signature, so the dishes you read about online may not be on the menu when you visit. The room is calm and green, with a level of service that matches the ambition of the food. It is a strong choice for a celebration in Atlanta.</p>

<p><strong>Dai Due – Austin</strong></p>
<p><strong>Cuisine</strong>: American<br />
<strong>Location</strong>: Cherrywood, Austin<br />
<strong>Ideal for</strong>: Impressing visitors, romantic evenings, brunch<br />
<strong>Description</strong>: Dai Due is dedicated to the concept of locality with ingredients exclusively sourced locally. The menu, which changes every evening, features brined pork chops, 80-day aged ribeyes, and wild game. The semi-formal ambiance complements an authentically Texan dining experience.</p>

<p>The restaurant is also a butcher shop and a bakery, which tells you most of what you need to know about its approach: whole-animal cooking, Texas produce, and a menu written around what is available that week. It is one of the best places in the state to understand what "local" actually means when it is taken seriously.</p>

<p><strong>Kata Robata – Houston</strong></p>
<p><strong>Cuisine</strong>: Japanese<br />
<strong>Location</strong>: Upper Kirby, Houston<br />
<strong>Ideal for</strong>: Business dinners, romantic evenings, dining with parents, special occasions<br />
<strong>Description</strong>: Kata Robata operates at a level of consistent excellence with a wide range of sushi and izakaya dishes. Every dish, whether it is an omakase at the sushi bar or a bowl of ramen, is prepared with impeccable quality.</p>

<p>It is a rare restaurant that can handle both a serious sushi counter and a busy izakaya menu without compromising either. The room is comfortable and well suited to a business dinner, and the service is used to guests who want guidance rather than a lecture. Book the sushi bar if you can.</p>

<h3>How to book and what to expect</h3>

<p><strong>Reservations.</strong> Most of these restaurants use Tock, Resy, or OpenTable, and the best tasting-menu rooms release seats weeks or months in advance. Set a reminder for the day bookings open; for the smallest counters, that is the difference between eating there and not.</p>

<p><strong>Cancellation policies.</strong> Tasting menus often require a card and charge a fee for late cancellation or no-shows. Read the policy before you book, especially if you are planning a trip around the reservation.</p>

<p><strong>Tipping.</strong> Unlike in France, service is not included in the US. A tip of around 18–22% is standard, and it is a significant part of staff income. Budget for it when comparing prices.</p>

<p><strong>Dress.</strong> Most of these rooms are smart casual. A few, such as the more formal tasting menus, will suit a jacket, but very few American restaurants enforce a dress code. When in doubt, ask when you book.</p>

<p><strong>Timing.</strong> Dinner reservations usually run from about 5:30pm to 9:30pm. Walk-ins are possible at some places, particularly at the bar, but the highest-rated rooms are effectively reservation-only.</p>

<p>These restaurants represent the best of the American culinary scene, offering unique experiences and memorable dishes across various styles and cultures.</p>
