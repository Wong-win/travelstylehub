<p>On October 24, 2018, I had the opportunity to fly with Air France for the first time. My last-minute flight from Amsterdam to Madrid was an experience that ultimately led me to write a detailed review of the airline. Often, these reviews are written following a disastrous experience, but in my case, it was quite the opposite.</p>

<p>Air France is the French flag carrier and one of the largest airlines in Europe, with a short-haul network built around its hub at Paris Charles de Gaulle. Most travellers encounter it in economy on a two-hour intra-European hop, which is exactly the kind of flight I took. That makes this review useful if you are deciding whether to book Air France over a low-cost carrier for a short trip — the trade-offs are less about the plane and more about baggage rules, connections, and how the airline handles problems.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1200" src="/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/jeshoots-com-227882-unsplash-1024x683-1.jpg" alt="" width="1024" height="683" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/jeshoots-com-227882-unsplash-1024x683-1.jpg 1024w , /wp-content/uploads/2024/06/jeshoots-com-227882-unsplash-1024x683-1-300x200.jpg 300w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></p>

<h3>Departure</h3>

<p>My flight was scheduled at an ungodly hour, 6:45am, which meant I had to be at the airport at 4:45am. My journey to the airport was an adventure in itself, but that deserves another article entirely. I had barely slept, being awake for 43 hours straight, except for a few hours of sleep on the plane and short dozes here and there while waiting at the airport.</p>

<p>Early-morning departures are common on Air France's short-haul network because the airline uses the first wave of the day to feed its Paris hub. The upside is that you arrive at your destination with the whole day ahead of you. The downside is that airport transport in most European cities is thin at that hour, and the first security queues can be surprisingly long because several flights leave at once.</p>

<p>If you are connecting through Paris, give yourself more time than you think you need. Charles de Gaulle is a large airport with separate halls, and a tight connection can involve a shuttle bus even within the same terminal complex.</p>

<h3>Check-in and Baggage</h3>

<p>It was my first time with Air France, a typical European airline in terms of baggage policies: one carry-on bag and one personal item, not exceeding a combined weight of 12 kg. Being a carry-on-only traveler, I had to adapt, as my suitcase exceeded this limit. I ended up adding checked luggage online for 30 euros, saving 10 euros compared to adding it at the airport.</p>

<p>That price difference is worth remembering: on most European carriers, adding a bag during online check-in is cheaper than paying at the counter, sometimes by a significant margin. The 12 kg combined limit is also stricter than it sounds once you add a laptop bag, and it is enforced more consistently than on some low-cost airlines.</p>

<p>Check-in went smoothly and without any issues. The service agents were incredibly friendly, even at 4 in the morning. Their energy at that early hour is something I still don't understand.</p>

<p>One practical point for anyone flying Air France short-haul today: allowances and fees change, so check the current rules for your fare type before you pack. The basic economy fare on many European routes is more restrictive than the standard fare, particularly on checked bags and seat selection.</p>

<h3>The Flight</h3>

<p>The economy class flight on Air France doesn't offer many frills. Business class passengers enjoy refreshing towels, a meal, and free champagne, even on short intra-European routes. In economy class, I received a sandwich, a cookie, and a drink on each segment of my flight (there was a stopover in Paris).</p>

<p>That snack service is one of the clearer differences between Air France and a typical low-cost carrier. On a two-hour flight it is not a meal, but it is a small, civilised touch that makes the trip feel less transactional. The seats on the short-haul fleet are standard European economy: adequate for a two-hour hop, tight if you are tall.</p>

<p>I fell asleep before even taking off towards Paris and woke up just long enough to eat my sandwich. The flight lasted about an hour. Once at Charles de Gaulle Airport, I headed to my gate, double and triple checking that I was in the right place.</p>

<h3>Gate Change</h3>

<p>Unfortunately, the gate number changed without me noticing. When I showed up at the boarding gate, I was told that the flight was to Lisbon, while I was waiting for Madrid. Completely exhausted after 26 hours without sleep, I headed to customer service.</p>

<p>The service agent confirmed that my flight had already left and the gate number had changed. Understanding my situation, she changed my flight to the next departure at no additional cost and assured me that my luggage would be transferred and waiting for me in Madrid.</p>

<p>This is the part of the story that matters most for anyone evaluating an airline. Gate changes happen everywhere, and a tired traveller missing one is not unusual. What made the difference was how the airline handled it: no argument, no fee, and a clear explanation of what would happen to the bag. That is not guaranteed on every carrier, and it is worth weighing when the fare difference between Air France and a low-cost alternative is small.</p>

<h3>Waiting at the Airport</h3>

<p>With several hours to kill before my new flight, I regretted not having explored the Air France lounge. These lounges offer showers, free wifi, a buffet, and drinks for a reasonable cost. It would have allowed me to rest more comfortably and freshen up before continuing my journey.</p>

<p>Air France sells paid access to its lounges at Paris Charles de Gaulle, and on a long connection it can be better value than it first appears: a shower, a quiet seat, and unlimited coffee can make the difference between arriving refreshed and arriving wrecked. Access rules vary by terminal and by fare, so check before you rely on it.</p>

<p>For a shorter wait, the terminals at Charles de Gaulle have a reasonable choice of cafés and seating, though power sockets are heavily contested. A portable battery is worth carrying.</p>

<h3>The New Flight</h3>

<p>The following flight went without incident. The flight attendants were courteous and served us a sandwich and a cookie on the two-hour flight to Madrid. I appreciate this snack service, which American airlines would do well to imitate.</p>

<p>Boarding was calm and on time, and the cabin crew handled a full flight without fuss. The difference between a stressful connection and a pleasant one often comes down to this kind of small operational competence rather than anything dramatic.</p>

<h3>Arrival in Madrid</h3>

<p>Upon arrival in Madrid, I discovered that my luggage had stayed in Paris. I filled out the necessary paperwork with slight pessimism, expecting never to see my suitcase again. However, to my great surprise, my luggage was delivered to me the next morning.</p>

<p>Delayed baggage is one of the most common problems in air travel, and the outcome here was better than average: the bag arrived the following morning, delivered to the address I gave. If this happens to you, keep the baggage report reference, keep receipts for anything you had to buy, and check what your fare and the applicable regulations entitle you to claim.</p>

<h3>What to know before flying Air France short-haul</h3>

<p><strong>Baggage.</strong> The carry-on allowance is typically one cabin bag plus one personal item, with a combined weight limit. Check the exact rules for your fare, and add checked bags online rather than at the airport.</p>

<p><strong>Connections at Paris.</strong> Charles de Gaulle is a big hub. Allow at least 90 minutes for a short-haul connection, more if you are changing halls or terminals. Follow the connection signs rather than exiting, and keep your boarding pass accessible.</p>

<p><strong>Delays and cancellations.</strong> Flights within the EU are covered by EU Regulation 261/2004. In broad terms, if your flight is delayed by three hours or more at arrival, cancelled with short notice, or you are denied boarding, you may be entitled to compensation, and the airline must also provide assistance such as meals and accommodation where applicable. Keep your boarding pass and any receipts.</p>

<p><strong>Food and drink.</strong> Economy short-haul includes a small snack and a drink on most Air France routes. If you have dietary requirements, eat before you fly or bring something with you.</p>

<p><strong>Lounges.</strong> Paid access is available at Paris, and it is worth considering on a long connection. Access rules depend on your fare and status.</p>

<h3>Conclusion</h3>

<p>Despite a few inconveniences, my overall experience with Air France was positive. The customer service was excellent, and the airline kept its promises by delivering my luggage quickly. I highly recommend Air France and will not hesitate to fly with them in the future.</p>

<p>The honest summary is that Air France short-haul economy is not luxurious, and it is not meant to be. What it offers is a functional, reasonably comfortable flight, a small snack, and — on this occasion at least — staff who solved a problem quickly and without charging for it. For a short European trip where the fare is competitive, that is a reasonable trade.</p>
