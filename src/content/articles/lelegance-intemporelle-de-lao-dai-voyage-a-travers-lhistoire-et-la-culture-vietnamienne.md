<p>The &#193;o d&#224;i, Vietnam&#8217;s national garment, is one of the country&#8217;s most striking symbols of beauty. Over the centuries, the &#225;o d&#224;i has evolved alongside Vietnam, going from royal attire to practical garment, from humble to haute couture, and then back into fashion. The Vietnamese &#225;o d&#224;i is beloved by young and old alike, and its elegant lines flatter both men and women. If you&#8217;re interested in learning more about the history of the &#225;o d&#224;i and getting tips for making your own, keep reading.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1322" src="http://travelstylehub.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/traditional-clothes-vietnam_1.jpg" alt="" width="870" height="580" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/traditional-clothes-vietnam_1.jpg 870w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/07/traditional-clothes-vietnam_1-300x200.jpg 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/07/traditional-clothes-vietnam_1-768x512.jpg 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/07/traditional-clothes-vietnam_1-480x320.jpg 480w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/07/traditional-clothes-vietnam_1-280x186.jpg 280w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/07/traditional-clothes-vietnam_1-600x400.jpg 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/07/traditional-clothes-vietnam_1-585x390.jpg 585w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 870px) 100vw, 870px" /></p>
<p>The history of the &#225;o d&#224;i begins in 1744, when Vietnam was divided into two territories, &#272;&#224;ng Trong (the Inner Land) and &#272;&#224;ng Ngo&#224;i (the Outer Land). To distinguish his people, Lord Nguy&#7877;n Ph&#250;c Kho&#225;t of the Inner Land required his subjects to wear a front-buttoned gown with trousers. This five-part robe (&#225;o ng&#361; th&#226;n) inspired the modern &#225;o d&#224;i. Royalty and elites crafted their robes from the finest silks, with intricate details and vibrant colors to represent ranks at court.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1323" src="http://travelstylehub.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Vietnam-national-costume.jpg" alt="" width="870" height="580" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Vietnam-national-costume.jpg 870w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Vietnam-national-costume-300x200.jpg 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Vietnam-national-costume-768x512.jpg 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Vietnam-national-costume-480x320.jpg 480w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Vietnam-national-costume-280x186.jpg 280w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Vietnam-national-costume-600x400.jpg 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Vietnam-national-costume-585x390.jpg 585w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 870px) 100vw, 870px" /></p>
<p>In the 1930s, the robe was simplified into two parts by the Vietnamese artist Le Mur Nguy&#7877;n C&#225;t T&#432;&#7901;ng. The front panel was lengthened to reach the ankle, and the shape became more fitted. As with many &#8220;Westernized&#8221; ideas, the style was initially met with reluctance. However, after Le Mur designed an &#225;o d&#224;i collection for Queen Nam Ph&#432;&#417;ng, wife of Vietnam&#8217;s last emperor B&#7843;o &#272;&#7841;i, urban women began adopting and popularizing the more contemporary style.</p>
<p>After the establishment of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam, H&#7891; Ch&#237; Minh wrote an essay in 1947 noting that the &#225;o d&#224;i was not suited to the fields or factories, asking the Vietnamese people to adapt their clothing to be more efficient at work. As a result, the &#225;o d&#224;i became a robe reserved for professional appearances and special occasions. Nowadays, across the country, high school girls are required to wear the &#225;o d&#224;i at least one day per week. Girls in white &#225;o d&#224;i riding their bicycles to school have inspired countless Vietnamese songs, poems, and paintings. The &#225;o d&#224;i is also worn by women of all ages whenever it&#8217;s time to make a good impression.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1324" src="http://travelstylehub.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/vietnam-costume.jpg" alt="" width="870" height="580" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/vietnam-costume.jpg 870w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/07/vietnam-costume-300x200.jpg 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/07/vietnam-costume-768x512.jpg 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/07/vietnam-costume-480x320.jpg 480w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/07/vietnam-costume-280x186.jpg 280w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/07/vietnam-costume-600x400.jpg 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/07/vietnam-costume-585x390.jpg 585w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 870px) 100vw, 870px" /></p>
<p>TIP: Visit the &#225;o d&#224;i collection on permanent display at the Vietnamese Women&#8217;s Museum in Hanoi to learn more about the history of this robe and its many variations.</p>
<p>Modern &#225;o d&#224;i styles are varied. Boat neck, shortened length, short sleeves — modern designers are not afraid to explore creative avenues when it comes to the &#225;o d&#224;i. Some of these new takes on the traditional robe make it more practical, meaning it can be worn in less formal settings. Knee-length styles are handy for motorbike and bicycle rides, and you&#8217;ll see a variety of more casual necklines worn on summer days, including round neck and boat neck styles.</p>
<p>Vietnamese women love wearing the &#225;o d&#224;i for photoshoots in beautiful settings. Golden autumn days in Hanoi call for photoshoots around Ho&#224;n Ki&#7871;m Lake. During T&#7871;t (Lunar New Year), you&#8217;ll see the robe in all its glory as women wear it to visit friends and family, and to pay respects at temples and shrines. A wedding is the perfect occasion to wear your finest &#225;o d&#224;i. In Vietnam, the bride and groom as well as the wedding party wear elaborate &#225;o d&#224;i during the traditional ceremony, then guests arrive at the reception in simpler yet equally colorful designs.</p>
<p>TIP: For a glimpse into the &#225;o d&#224;i&#8217;s place in Vietnamese fashion, watch the movie &#8220;The Tailor&#8221; about an &#225;o d&#224;i fashion house in Ho Chi Minh City. The film was Vietnam&#8217;s official entry for Best Foreign Language Film at the 2018 Oscars.</p>
<p>The colors of the &#225;o d&#224;i each carry meaning. In imperial times, gold was a royal color reserved for kings and queens. Red &#225;o d&#224;i are worn during T&#7871;t for luck and prosperity. Brides and grooms may also wear red on their wedding day, so it&#8217;s wise to choose another color if you&#8217;re a guest. White symbolizes purity and innocence, as seen in the white robes of high school girls, and black is most commonly worn at funerals. Vietnamese women also choose &#225;o d&#224;i colors based on their element (metal, wood, water, fire, or earth) based on their birth year.</p>
<p>Anyone can wear an &#225;o d&#224;i as a sign of respect. Gala dinners, important meetings, and celebrations are all appropriate occasions — just be sure to always wear your &#225;o d&#224;i over trousers or bottoms. If you&#8217;re looking for a ready-to-wear &#225;o d&#224;i, you&#8217;ll be spoiled for choice in the shops of Hanoi&#8217;s Old Quarter and H&#7897;i An ancient town. H&#7897;i An tailors can also make you an entirely new robe in just a few days. Don&#8217;t hesitate to choose your own style and colors to add a personal touch. To create a custom &#225;o d&#224;i, visit V&#7841;n Ph&#250;c silk village, 20 minutes from Hanoi. There, you can buy high-quality silk at the source, learn more about silk production, have your &#225;o d&#224;i custom-made, and take gorgeous photos among the floating silk ribbons.</p>
<p>The traditional Vietnamese garment, the &#225;o d&#224;i, is not just a piece of clothing, but a profound symbol of the country&#8217;s culture and history. Every detail of the &#225;o d&#224;i, from its cut to its color, tells a story rich in meaning and tradition. Through its evolutions, the &#225;o d&#224;i remains a living testament to Vietnamese elegance, reflecting both ancient heritage and modern influences. Whether for a ceremony, a special occasion, or simply to celebrate the beauty of Vietnamese culture, wearing an &#225;o d&#224;i is a way to connect deeply with the soul of Vietnam.</p>
