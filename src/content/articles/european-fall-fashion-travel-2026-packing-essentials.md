Packing for a European autumn trip is harder than packing for summer. Summer has one rule: light, breathable, repeat. Autumn has weather that changes its mind every six hours, dress codes that range from Milan gallery openings to Lisbon seafood lunches, and the eternal question of whether to bring heels (you should not).

After two decades of European travel — and one wardrobe disaster in a Parisian rainstorm that I will never live down — here is the packing list that finally works for a multi-city European autumn trip. Carry-on only. Ten days. Three countries.

## The 12-piece formula

### Outerwear (2 pieces, both worn)

1. **A mid-weight wool coat in camel or charcoal** — works over everything, from jeans to dresses to evening trousers. Choose one that fits your shoulders perfectly; a tailor can take in the body if needed.
2. **A packable rain shell** — black or navy, lightweight, with a hood. The Decathlon Quechua at €40 outperforms most €200 options. You'll forget you have it until you need it desperately.

### Tops (4 pieces)

3. **Cashmere crewneck sweater** in cream or navy — worn 6+ times across the trip
4. **Fine merino turtleneck** in black — for layering under everything, including the cashmere sweater on cold days
5. **Silk or cotton button-down** in white — works for dinners, museums, and unexpected nicer-than-expected restaurants
6. **Long-sleeve tee in black or grey** — the ultimate base layer and travel-day staple

### Bottoms (3 pieces)

7. **Dark indigo straight-leg jeans** — wear them on the plane, they recover from sitting
8. **Tailored wool trousers in black** — dress up or down, work for any evening
9. **A midi-length skirt in a seasonless fabric** (silk, wool crepe, or ponte) — the secret weapon for variety

### Dress (1 piece)

10. **A slip dress in black or burgundy** — under the cashmere sweater by day, alone with ankle boots for evening. The single most versatile piece in the bag.

### Shoes (3 pairs, all worn on the plane)

11. **White leather sneakers** — Veja, Common Projects, or Stan Smith. Worn for travel day to save bag space.
12. **Ankle boots** — block heel, almond toe, leather. Replace heels entirely.
13. **Foldable ballet flats** — black leather, pack flat. For the slip dress, dinners, and when your feet need a break from the boots.

### Accessories (5 small pieces that transform everything)

14. **A silk scarf** — large, in a print you love. Tied around the neck, on a bag handle, or as a hair tie.
15. **A leather belt** in dark brown — same color as your boots
16. **A structured tote bag** — black or camel leather, holds a water bottle and a notebook
17. **A small crossbody bag** — for evenings and crowded tourist sites
18. **A simple watch or bracelet** — pick one; do not wear jewelry that needs to come off at every security checkpoint

## The colors that mix

The whole system works because every piece is in the same colour palette: black, navy/cream, camel, dark indigo, with one accent (burgundy or olive). This means every top goes with every bottom, and you can dress up or down any combination without thinking.

The single piece to skip: anything in a bold print. It limits your combinations and photographs badly next to European architecture.

## What stays home

- **Heels over 5cm**: you'll walk 15,000 steps a day. The cobblestones will punish you.
- **White jeans**: Paris rain, Milan espresso, Lisbon seafood — all stains.
- **A "going out" top**: if you can't wear it during the day, it doesn't earn suitcase space.
- **More than one dress**: the slip dress works for both dinners.
- **Hair tools**: every European hotel has a hair dryer. Most have reasonably good ones.
- **More than 4 pairs of shoes**: see above re: cobblestones.

## The 4-3-2-1 method for packing

For any multi-city European trip:

- **4 tops** (mix of long-sleeve, sweater, button-down)
- **3 bottoms** (jeans, trousers, skirt)
- **2 layers** (coat + rain shell)
- **1 dress** (slip dress, your most versatile piece)

This system gives you 24 possible outfits from 10 pieces. Mathematically, you cannot wear the same outfit twice in 10 days unless you're really trying.

## The 10-day European autumn itinerary that justifies the list

**Days 1-4: Paris** — 12°C, rain every third day. Wear the coat + cashmere sweater + jeans + ankle boots. Galleries, bistros, walks along the Seine. Bring the rain shell on every outing.

**Days 5-7: Milan** — 18°C, mostly dry. Lighter outfits — turtleneck + trousers, slip dress with boots. A dinner at a gallery opening? Slip dress alone with the scarf as a wrap.

**Days 8-10: Lisbon** — 22°C, sunny. Roll up the sleeves of the button-down, swap the boots for sneakers, eat seafood lunches by the Tagus river. The wool coat stays at the hotel.

## The secret weapon: laundry

Every European city has a same-day laundry service (pressing only, often delivered to your hotel in 4 hours) for €15-25/kg. Schedule one mid-trip — usually on day 5 or 6 when you've worn everything at least once — and you'll come home with a lighter suitcase than you started with.

This means you can pack for 10 days in 12 pieces and still feel like you're wearing fresh clothes every day. The Europeans figured this out decades ago. American tourists dragging 23kg hard-shell suitcases through the métro are paying for the privilege of hauling clean clothes they'll never wear.

## The final tip

Wear your heaviest pieces on the plane. Coat, boots, sweater, jeans — all of them on you, not in the bag. This saves 4-5kg of bag weight, which means you can pack more, and you arrive looking polished, not rumpled.

The carry-on only European trip isn't about minimalism. It's about travelling smarter. Pack the 12-piece formula, trust the system, and spend your energy on the experiences, not on outfit anxiety.
