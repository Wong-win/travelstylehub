<p>The Douro is an iconic region of Portugal, famous for its terraced vineyards that produce the renowned Port wine. One of the best ways to discover this magnificent valley is to take the Linha do Douro train from Porto to Pocinho. This picturesque journey is considered one of the most beautiful train trips in Europe, offering spectacular views over the Douro Valley.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1106" src="/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Lonely-Planet-Linha-do-Douro-8533.avif" alt="" width="1920" height="1282" /></p>
<h3>Departure from Porto</h3>
<p>To fully enjoy this trip, it is recommended to take the first train of the day, which departs from São Bento station at 7:15 am. This station is an attraction in itself, famous for its magnificent azulejos, hand-painted ceramic tiles depicting historical scenes.</p>
<p>If you wish to avoid changing trains at Ermesinde, take the first direct Linha do Douro service at 8:20 am. This journey takes you through the grey and semi-industrial suburbs of Porto before reaching the greener countryside after Caíde.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1105" src="/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Lonely-Planet-Linha-do-Douro-8442.avif" alt="" width="1920" height="1282" /></p>
<h3>The Journey to Pinhão</h3>
<p>After about an hour of travel, you will glimpse the first views of the Douro at Ribadouro. From this point on, the train winds along the river, passing through terraced vineyards and picturesque villages clinging to the hillsides. In January, the mist and fog can add a mysterious and isolated atmosphere to the landscape.</p>
<p>The first interesting stop is the town of Pinhão, where you arrive around 10 am (or 11 am if you take the direct train). Pinhão is a charming little town known for its ceramic tiles decorating the station. After a stroll along the river, it is time to think about lunch.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1107" src="/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Lonely-Planet-Linha-do-Douro-8707.avif" alt="" width="1920" height="1282" /></p>
<h3>Wine Tasting and Lunch in Pinhão</h3>
<p>Pinhão offers several lunch options. You can choose from riverside restaurants such as Veladoura or Cozinha da Clara, or head to Talho Qualifer – Quinta das Barrocas to sample smoked meats and prepare a picnic by the river.</p>
<p>In the heart of Portugal&#8217;s most prestigious wine region, a visit to Pinhão would be incomplete without a wine tasting. Houses such as Croft, Quinta do Bomfim, and Quinta do Noval offer standard and fortified wine tastings.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1108" src="/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Lonely-Planet-Linha-do-Douro-8719-1.avif" alt="" width="1920" height="1282" /></p>
<h3>The Journey to Pocinho</h3>
<p>After enjoying Pinhão for four or five hours, it is time to continue towards Pocinho by taking the 3:43 pm train. This final stretch of the journey is arguably the most beautiful, with increasingly wild and rugged landscapes.</p>
<p>The train crosses numerous bridges and runs alongside abandoned vineyards and tiny stations that lead to hiking trails. As you approach Pocinho, the landscape opens up to reveal a valley lined with olive trees, with a dam and a power station in the distance.</p>
<h3>Return to Porto</h3>
<p>At Pocinho, the original wooden station is still intact, but there are few other attractions. Take the return train to Porto at 5:14 pm, and if all goes well, you should arrive in Porto around 8:30 pm, just in time for dinner.</p>
<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<p><strong>When is the best time to go?</strong> The weather in January can be wet and foggy, but for clear views and longer days, it is better to travel in summer.</p>
<p><strong>Where can you eat?</strong> Pinhão is the ideal place to eat and drink. If you are on a tight budget, prepare a picnic in advance.</p>
<p><strong>Where can you buy tickets?</strong> It is possible to buy tickets in advance or on the day at the station. Round-trip tickets cost around €30, and buying separate tickets for each leg of the journey costs slightly more.</p>
<p>This train trip through the Douro Valley is an unforgettable experience that offers a unique glimpse into one of Portugal&#8217;s most picturesque regions. Whether you are a wine lover or simply looking for beautiful scenery, this train journey is an excellent way to discover the natural beauty and wine culture of the region.</p>
