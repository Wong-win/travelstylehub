<p>New Jersey, known as the &#8220;Diner Capital of the World&#8221;, is home to around 500 of these iconic establishments. They reflect the diversity of the local population and their rich history. Here is a selection of the best diners in this state, where every visit promises a unique culinary experience.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1254" src="http://travelstylehub.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Summit-diner-New-Jersey.avif" alt="" width="1920" height="1523" /></p>
<p><strong>The History of Diners</strong></p>
<p>The history of diners begins in Providence, Rhode Island, in 1872, with Walter Scott who sold meals from the back of a horse-drawn cart. These mobile establishments quickly gained popularity throughout New England. In New Jersey, entrepreneurs like Jerry O&#8217;Mahony transformed these concepts into permanent restaurants built from mass-produced railroad cars. These diners catered to a working-class population seeking affordable meals 24 hours a day, along the state&#8217;s developing roads.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1253" src="http://travelstylehub.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/New-Jersey-Skylark-Diner-Courtesy-Suzanne-Caputo-RM.avif" alt="" width="1920" height="915" /></p>
<p><strong>Diners of Yesterday and Today</strong></p>
<p>New Jersey diners thrived throughout the 20th century, becoming not only places where you could eat inexpensively, but also roadside attractions in their own right, thanks to their neon-lit metallic aesthetic that developed after World War II. Today, although not all diners retain this retro style, they remain very popular thanks to their varied menus covering everything from all-day breakfasts to burgers, Greek specialties, and homemade pies.</p>
<p><strong>Summit Diner – Summit</strong> Summit Diner, opened in 1928, is considered the oldest still-operating diner in New Jersey. Its current building, built in 1939 by Jerry O&#8217;Mahony, features the classic railroad car look. It is said that Ernest Hemingway frequented this establishment, although this may not be true. This authentic New Jersey diner offers very popular mini pork roll, egg, and cheese sandwiches.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1257" src="http://travelstylehub.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Georges-Place-067-RM.avif" alt="" width="1920" height="1010" /></p>
<p><strong>Jefferson Diner – Lake Hopatcong</strong> Despite opening in 1985, Jefferson Diner embraces the retro diner aesthetic with its neon lights and chrome exterior. It stands out for its generous portions and large seating capacity. It was featured on a Guy Fieri show and is now home to Fieri&#8217;s 1967 Camaro SS.</p>
<p><strong>Skylark Fine Diner and Lounge – Edison</strong> Opened in 2006, the Skylark Fine Diner and Lounge is one of the new types of New Jersey diners. It offers a wide variety of classic American and international dishes in a modern 80s-inspired setting. This diner became famous thanks to an episode of Jerry Seinfeld&#8217;s &#8220;Comedians in Cars Getting Coffee&#8221;.</p>
<p><strong>Broad Street Diner – Keyport</strong> Designed by Jerry O&#8217;Mahony, the Broad Street Diner has undergone several name and ownership changes over its 70-year history, but its 50s visual style remains intact. This diner is famous for dishes like chocolate babka French toast and crab-stuffed lobster tails. Coffee is brewed with beans from a private roaster.</p>
<p><strong>George&#8217;s Place – Cape May</strong> Founded in 1968, George&#8217;s Place in Cape May stands out for its Greek atmosphere and its absence of traditional New Jersey diner kitsch decor. This diner offers popular Greek dishes like gyros, falafels, and pita, in a warm setting with green tables and tiled walls.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1255" src="http://travelstylehub.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/tiktok-diner-new-jersey-facade-RM.avif" alt="" width="1920" height="640" /></p>
<p><strong>Mustache Bill&#8217;s – Barnegat Light</strong> Mustache Bill&#8217;s, open since 1972, is known for its pancakes. The current owner, Bill Smith, makes all dishes from scratch, which earned him a distinction from the James Beard Foundation in 2009. His most famous dish is the &#8220;Cyclops&#8221;, a pancake with an egg in the middle.</p>
<p><strong>Tick Tock Diner – Clifton</strong> Opened in 1948, the Tick Tock Diner is a true New Jersey diner, open 24 hours a day, 365 days a year. Its classic neon exterior was modernized in 2019 with an art deco touch. The menu, while retaining traditional dishes, also offers &#8220;elevated&#8221; options like scallops. Mick Jagger even expressed his love for this diner.</p>
<p><strong>Tops Diner – East Newark</strong> Tops Diner, opened in 1942, underwent a controversial $11 million renovation in 2021, which eliminated the worn diner aesthetic. Despite this transformation, its acclaimed international menu remains largely the same. The lobster mac and cheese is a must-have.</p>
<p><strong>Conclusion</strong></p>
<p>New Jersey&#8217;s diners are more than just a place to eat. They are witnesses to the state&#8217;s social and cultural history. Each diner has its own story and its own atmosphere, offering a unique experience to every visitor. Whether you&#8217;re looking for giant portions, nostalgia, or Greek dishes, there&#8217;s a diner for you in New Jersey.</p>
