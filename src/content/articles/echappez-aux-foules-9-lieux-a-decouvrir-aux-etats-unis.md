<p>With its vast expanses and diversity of landscapes, the United States offers many opportunities to find solitude. However, some of the most beautiful places in the country can be overrun with visitors, especially during the peak summer season. Here are nine of the best places in the United States to escape the crowds.</p>
<h4>1. Capitol Reef National Park, Utah</h4>
<p>Recommended by Deepa Lakshmin</p>
<p>Capitol Reef, one of Utah’s five great national parks, is surprisingly quiet. The Sulfur Creek and Cohab Canyon trails offer serene hikes. Sulfur Creek, with its canyons and waterfalls, is particularly wild and untouched. Torrey, UT, is a good base for exploring the park with accommodation options like the Skyview Hotel and restaurants like the Wild Rabbit Cafe and Hunt &amp; Gather.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1129" src="http://travelstylehub.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/shutterstock306206576.avif" alt="" width="1920" height="1226" /></p>
<h4>2. Montana Badlands</h4>
<p>Recommended by Bailey Freeman</p>
<p>The Badlands of eastern Montana are perfect for those looking to get away. With stunning landscapes, dinosaur fossils, and star-filled skies, the Badlands offer a unique experience without the crowds. The pyramidal mounds of the Terry Badlands and the cliffs of Makoshika State Park are ideal spots for hiking and camping.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1128" src="http://travelstylehub.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/GettyImages-523410044.avif" alt="" width="1920" height="1280" /></p>
<h4>3. Coldfoot, Alaska</h4>
<p>Recommended by Erin Kirkland</p>
<p>Coldfoot, Alaska, is a perfect place to experience the remote spaces of the north. Located at the base of the Brooks Range, Coldfoot offers year-round activities like hiking, rafting, and northern lights viewing. Coldfoot Camp offers guided excursions to the area’s national parks and preserves.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1127" src="http://travelstylehub.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/GettyImages-468009450.avif" alt="" width="1920" height="1281" /></p>
<h4>4. Hovenweep National Monument, Four Corners, USA</h4>
<p>Recommended by Liz Prado</p>
<p>Hovenweep National Monument is a little-visited archaeological area in the Four Corners region. This site was once home to the Ancestral Puebloans and features six ancient villages. The Square Tower group is the largest and most accessible, with well-preserved structures and trails to explore the surroundings.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1133" src="http://travelstylehub.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/shutterstock2235760517.avif" alt="" width="1920" height="1281" /></p>
<h4>5. Superstition Mountains, Arizona</h4>
<p>Recommended by Melissa Yeager</p>
<p>The Superstition Mountains, east of Phoenix, offer spectacular hikes with desert landscapes and saguaro cacti. Trails like the Hieroglyphic Trail and Fremont Saddle are perfect for hikers of all levels. The summit of Flat Iron offers a stunning view for experienced hikers.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1132" src="http://travelstylehub.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/usa-shenandoah-GettyImages-1094518378-rfc.avif" alt="" width="1920" height="1280" /></p>
<h4>6. Shenandoah Valley, Virginia</h4>
<p>Recommended by Ann Douglas Lott</p>
<p>The Shenandoah Valley, surrounded by the Blue Ridge and Alleghany Mountains, is a 140-mile long paradise. The winding roads of the Blue Ridge Parkway and Skyline Drive offer magnificent scenery. The valley is ideal for romantic getaways with vineyards, farms, and unforgettable sunsets.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1134" src="http://travelstylehub.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/GettyImages-1390131157.avif" alt="" width="1920" height="1279" /></p>
<h4>7. Bellingham, Washington</h4>
<p>Recommended by Chamidae Ford</p>
<p>Bellingham, in Washington State, is a peaceful oasis with many outdoor activities. Chuckanut Drive offers stunning views of Bellingham Bay and hikes like Oyster Dome. In town, restaurants like Mambo Italiano and independent bookstores like Village Books add to Bellingham’s charm.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1135" src="http://travelstylehub.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/CantoiStock-1284840905-RFC.avif" alt="" width="1920" height="1281" /></p>
<h4>8. Channel Islands National Park, California</h4>
<p>Recommended by Brekke Fletcher</p>
<p>Channel Islands National Park is an isolated destination accessible by boat from Ventura or Oxnard. The park’s five islands offer wild landscapes and unique species of plants and animals. Campsites are available for extended stays and whale watching is possible from June to November.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1130" src="http://travelstylehub.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/shutterstock1266892087.avif" alt="" width="1920" height="1280" /></p>
<h4>9. Jayuya, Puerto Rico</h4>
<p>Recommended by John Garry</p>
<p>Jayuya, nestled in the central mountains of Puerto Rico, is an ideal place to discover the indigenous Taíno culture. Hikes around the Toro Negro State Forest and visits to coffee farms like Hacienda San Pedro offer unique experiences. Natural pools and waterfalls such as Cascada Las Delicias add to the charm of this region.</p>
<p>These nine destinations offer a variety of experiences for those looking to escape the crowds and discover lesser-visited places in the United States. Whether for hiking, camping, stargazing, or simply relaxing, these places promise moments of tranquility and natural beauty.</p>
