<h2>Ryanair’s Peace Deal with OTAs</h2>
<p>Ryanair has recently signed a series of peace deals with its former adversaries, including Love Holidays and Kiwi.com. This marks the end of legal battles that have often led to heated courtroom drama, particularly in the United States. This move appears to be driven by pragmatic reasons, given that the absence of Ryanair flights from OTA inventories has impacted the airline’s load factors.</p>
<h2>Hotel Bed Shortage in the Irish Holiday Market</h2>
<p>The Irish holiday market is suffering from a shortage of hotel beds. In 2023, around 2,600 beds were added, and a further 3,000 are expected by 2025. However, the sector has lost around 8,000 rooms to government contracts. Additionally, the short-term rental sector could be impacted by new regulations, which could significantly reduce the number of beds available to tourists.<img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-1169" src="/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/ryanair-4688654_1280-1.webp" alt="" width="1000" height="607" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/ryanair-4688654_1280-1.webp 1000w , /wp-content/uploads/2024/06/ryanair-4688654_1280-1-300x182.webp 300w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 1000px) 100vw, 1000px" /></p>
<h2>Dublin Airport News</h2>
<p>Dublin Airport has decided not to include transit passengers in its official figures for 2023. This could help keep the numbers below the 32 million passenger cap, but there is uncertainty about the actual figures. Ryanair, which does not allow transfers on a single booking reference, may contribute to an undercount of self-connected passengers.</p>
<h2>Cruises and Holiday World Show</h2>
<p>The cruise market is seeing a shift of ships away from the Red Sea and the eastern Mediterranean, which could benefit consumers with more favorable prices and bookings. The Holiday World Show in Dublin showed that Spain remains the preferred destination for Irish travelers, despite new routes to Greece and Italy.
