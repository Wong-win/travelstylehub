<p>You do not need new cabinets to change how a kitchen feels. Most of the difference comes from what is left on the counter, how the lighting falls, and whether the room looks cared for rather than merely used. A few deliberate changes can make a rental kitchen read as considered — without touching the tiles.</p>

<h2>Clear the counter, then put three things back</h2>

<p>The most effective styling move is subtraction. Clear everything off the worktop, wipe it down, and look at the space. Then put back only what you use daily, plus two or three objects you genuinely like looking at. A wooden board, a ceramic bowl, a small plant or a good knife block is enough.</p>

<p>Appliances that live on the counter — toaster, kettle, coffee machine — should be grouped in one zone rather than spread along the length of the worktop. Clustering them frees up a clear run of surface, which is what makes a kitchen look calm.</p>

<h2>Use open shelving carefully</h2>

<p>Open shelves can look beautiful or chaotic depending on what is on them. Keep the palette consistent: matching glasses, a few ceramic plates, one or two wooden pieces, a small plant. Avoid packing every shelf to the edge. Leaving a gap at the end of each shelf is what makes the display feel curated instead of like storage that lost its doors.</p>

<p>If you have closed cabinets, consider removing one door on a small section to create an open shelf. It costs nothing and is easy to reverse.</p>

<h2>Change the hardware</h2>

<p>Cabinet handles and knobs are the cheapest visible upgrade in a kitchen. Swapping dated pulls for simple brass, matte black or brushed steel ones takes an afternoon and transforms the cabinetry. Keep the old hardware in a bag so you can put it back before moving out.</p>

<p>Measure the hole spacing before ordering. Handles come in standard centres — 96 mm, 128 mm, 160 mm — and a mismatch means drilling new holes.</p>

<h2>Light the worktop, not the ceiling</h2>

<p>One bright ceiling light makes a kitchen look flat. Add a warm under-cabinet strip or a small lamp on an open shelf. The light bouncing off the worktop creates depth and makes the room feel welcoming after dark. Warm white (2700K) is the right choice here, as it flatters food and wood.</p>

<p>If you cannot wire anything, a rechargeable puck light or a plug-in strip is enough. This is one of the highest-impact, lowest-effort changes in the whole room.</p>

<h2>Choose one texture to repeat</h2>

<p>Wood, linen, ceramic, glass, marble. Pick one material and repeat it two or three times — a wooden board, wooden utensils, a wooden stool. Repetition is what makes a group of objects look intentional. Two textures are usually enough; three can start to feel busy in a small kitchen.</p>

<h2>Hide the clutter that has no style</h2>

<p>Bins, cleaning products, plastic containers and charging cables are not decor. Give them a home inside a cabinet, a drawer, or a lidded basket. If the kitchen has no storage, a simple tray on top of the fridge or a slim trolley beside the counter can absorb the overflow.</p>

<p>None of this requires a renovation. It requires an hour, a bin bag, and the willingness to leave some space empty. That empty space is what will make the room feel expensive.</p>
